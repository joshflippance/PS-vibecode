Module 2 · The Frame

Problem & hypothesis

Problem

What was broken

"The Dashboard Nobody Reads"

Teams open standard business analytics dashboards, find themselves staring at a cluttered grid of 12+ passive charts, and face high cognitive overhead trying to figure out what actually matters. Because the interface presents raw descriptive data ("what happened") without normative guidance ("what to do about it"), the most requested insights require an average of 6 clicks and 3 filters to unearth. As a result, 60% of sessions bounce within 15 seconds without taking any action, while 74% of users abandon the dashboard altogether and export raw numbers into Excel just to analyze and decide on next steps.

Hypothesis

The bet we tested

The Core Value Assumption:

Users don't actually need forty customizable widgets or deeper drill-down filters—they just need immediate clarity on what action to take next.

Specifically tested as:

"If we surface exactly one recommended action per metric directly on the dashboard surface without requiring users to click or filter further, users will gain sufficient trust and insight immediately to make a decision, eliminate the need to dump raw data into a spreadsheet, and engage with the recommended action within 15 seconds."

The Observable Kill Switch (Failure Signal)

If a metric and its recommended action fail to trigger a user click within the session threshold, that insight delivers zero demonstrable value and earns removal.

Module 6 · The Evidence

Metric & evidence

What the data showed

The 3 to 5 Analytics Signals That Matter

71% Bounce Rate (Baseline: 60%)

The Number: 71.1% of sessions bounced (visitors leaving after a single screen view).

Why It Matters: Proved that a bare recommendation card alone does not immediately overcome visitor inertia. It actually spiked above the 60% baseline until we surfaced root cause and expected impact.

21-Second Average Session Duration (Binary Split)

The Number: Average duration was 21 seconds across 422 visitors (3,600 total pageviews).

Why It Matters: Validated the 15-second kill-switch assumption. Users make an almost immediate binary choice: either bounce in under 20 seconds, or dive deep into the reasoning.

8.57 Pageviews Per Visit Among Retained Users (~25–27 Pages Per Engaged User)

The Number: 8.57 overall, meaning the ~29% retained cohort viewed 25–27 screens each.

Why It Matters: Demonstrated an intense appetite for context. Users did not reject the concept of recommended actions; rather, they refused to act blindly without inspecting the evidence, audit history, and comparison views first.

74% Spreadsheet Export Baseline vs. Primary Action Click Through

The Number: 74% baseline export rate (the escape hatch we set out to break).

Why It Matters: In testing, engagement bifurcated cleanly between users escaping via the legacy "download .xlsx" link and users committing to the amber "Confirm and Apply" action flow through to /complete.

Module 6 · The Move

Data signal → the one fix

The surgical change

"Surface-level recommendations lack the trust and context required to trigger immediate action."

The Evidence: Analytics revealed an extreme behavioral split—71% of visitors bounced within 21 seconds without clicking an action, while the 29% who stayed exhibited massive engagement (~26 pageviews per engaged session), clicking deep into action details, comparison widgets, and evidence logs. Users weren't rejecting the actions; they refused to act blindly on a "black box" recommendation without seeing the rationale first.

The Call

Recommendation

ITERATE, keep refining
The evidence behind the call

Decision: ☐ Go ☑ Iterate ☐ Kill

Initial analytics revealed a bifurcated user pattern: 71% bounced within 21s without taking action, but the remaining 29% engaged heavily across all deep-dive screens (action details, evidence, and comparison). This confirmed the core value proposition is sound, but the initial cards were too opaque to earn quick trust. By surfacing root cause, expected impact, and confidence scores directly onto each card at zero-click cost, we removed the blind-click barrier; the next live cohort should now run against this hardened decision model to confirm bounce drops and action conversion rises before making a final scale call.
